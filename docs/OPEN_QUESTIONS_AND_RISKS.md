## Legal and IP flags (not legal advice; tell the owner to check with a lawyer or accountant)

- **Securities and fundraising.** Avoid promising returns or a "use of proceeds" for holder money. Keep the airdrop copy as "Nothing is promised." Frame "Get in the Suit" as buying a feature.
- **Right of publicity and AI likeness.** Never put a real person's face in the suit except the buyer's own, with consent. No minors. Tennessee and New York have AI digital-replica laws; other states have right-of-publicity laws.
- **AI art copyright.** AI-only images are generally not copyrightable in the US. A human redraw is needed to own the character.
- **Third-party IP to avoid:**
  - the 1967 Patterson-Gimlin footage and frames (a generic walk or look-back pose is fine; don't trace it);
  - Pepe (Matt Furie), the Virgin/Chad drawings, Bored Ape, Monster Energy and Sully;
  - other characters' signature features;
  - NEAR's logo, used in a way that implies affiliation.
- **The "hoax" theme next to a coin** invites rug-pull jokes. Keep the token mechanics transparent: locked liquidity, public wallets, burns logged with transactions. The joke is on Bigfoot photos, never on buyers.
- **Tax and business entity:** get advice before using any existing LLC, especially a retirement-account-owned one, for this project.
